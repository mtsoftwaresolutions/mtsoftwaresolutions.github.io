HOW WE WORK

A clear process. Shared decisions.

Understand the need, agree the scope, review the work, and prepare for operation. The exact schedule and responsibilities are set for each project.

01Understand the need02Define scope and design the solution03Develop and review04Launch and support
  1. 01

    Understand the need

    Start with your idea, the problem you want to solve, and the people who will use the solution.

    Stage output
    A shared problem statement and user needs.
    Client review
    Confirm the workflow and desired outcome.
  2. 02

    Define scope and design the solution

    Discuss priorities, possible approaches, and what belongs in the project.

    Stage output
    A scope outline, priorities and solution design.
    Client review
    Agree what is included and how changes are handled.
  3. 03

    Develop and review

    Build the agreed solution and review how it serves the intended users.

    Stage output
    Working software and documented review findings.
    Client review
    Review user flows and agreed acceptance criteria.
  4. 04

    Launch and support

    Plan the release and discuss what is needed to keep the solution operating.

    Stage output
    A release plan, handover and operating requirements.
    Client review
    Confirm release readiness and separately agreed support coverage.

You bring the context.

You do not need a technical specification. A few details can make the first discussion more useful.

  • Your idea or current problem
  • Intended users or beneficiaries
  • Existing tools or systems
  • Your intended timeline, if known

Make room for the right people.

Agencies can discuss responsibilities and how their client will participate. Organizations can identify the people responsible for operations, decisions, and the systems involved. The next step is agreed around the project’s context.

A FEW USEFUL ANSWERS

Agree the details before delivery.

These questions guide the project agreement. Rights, coverage, costs and response arrangements need explicit terms; no fixed policy is assumed.

How do requirements become an agreed scope?
Identify user tasks, priorities, dependencies, included deliverables and exclusions. Agree review participants and acceptance criteria before implementation. A larger discovery effort may be scoped separately.
What will be tested and accepted?
Define the user journeys, supported environments, important failure cases and acceptance checks in the project scope. Record review findings and how they are resolved.
What documentation and handover are included?
List the required source files, setup instructions, release steps, account access and operating documents in the proposal. Identify who receives them and which environments must be ready.
Who owns the code and other assets?
Ownership, licensing, source delivery and rights to reusable or third-party components must be written into the agreement. Share any organization requirements before a proposal; no universal transfer policy is assumed.
Who handles hosting and provider costs?
Agree responsibility for hosting, domains, store accounts, licenses and other provider charges. Define operating access and recurring costs separately from development work.
What happens when requirements change?
Record the request and compare it with the agreed scope. Review its effort, cost, timing and dependencies before approval. Define how defects in accepted scope differ from new requirements in the agreement.
What does ongoing support cover?
Specify covered applications, environments, request types, capacity, exclusions and each party’s responsibilities. Response arrangements and support hours require explicit agreement.
How do I begin, and what happens next?
Share the need, users and current tools by email, phone or the form when available. The next discussion can clarify whether you need a scoping task or an implementation proposal.
How is an estimate prepared?
An estimate considers analysis, design, development, testing, project management, revisions and handover. Existing-system access and third-party costs also affect it. The proposal defines the included work and assumptions.
